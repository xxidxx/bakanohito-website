Abstract
As Indonesia pursues its net-zero 2060 target, PT Pertamina launched Pertamax Green 95  it remains available at only 177 of 6,000+ gas stations with weak consumer uptake. This study investigates the marketing strategy of Pertamax Green 95 in Indonesia, aiming to diagnose the causes of low adoption and design an evidence-based marketing strategy.
Using a mixed-methods approach, the study analyzes primary survey data (n = 124) from a structured 7Ps-based questionnaire alongside PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, and SWOT frameworks.
Findings reveal a marketing activation failure rather than a product deficiency. The overall 7Ps score of 3.449 ("Moderate") masks a severe internal asymmetry: Promotion (2.848) ranks far below Process (3.869) and Physical Evidence (3.851), with advertisement visibility (2.113) the lowest of all 32 items. Five interrelated problems are identified: promotional failure and brand invisibility, price-value asymmetry, distribution deficiency (60.5% cite availability as the primary barrier), digital underutilization, and green communication gap.

digital promotion, distribution densification to 30% network coverage, a Green Zone concept at retail stations, staff certification, and value-based pricing with trial incentives. Projected outcomes include improving the 7Ps mean from 3.449 to 3.65–3.70 and the Composite Loyalty Index from 3.552 to 3.80 within 12 months.

Key words: marketing strategy; 7Ps marketing mix; green fuel; Pertamax Green 95; STP strategy; bioethanol; Indonesia

Introduction
1.1 Research Background and Significance
1.1.1 Research Background
As part of its goal to reach net zero carbon emissions by 2060, Indonesia is undergoing a major energy transition. The transportation sector is a focal point, accounting for approximately 27% of the nation's greenhouse gas emissions, with road transport responsible for more than 80% of that share[1]. To accelerate decarbonization, the Indonesian government introduced Presidential Regulation No. 66/2018, mandating biodiesel blending targets (B30) and bioethanol blending (E2-E5) as part of a strategy to diversify the national energy mix[2,3]. A landmark initiative under this policy was PT Pertamina (Persero)'s launch of Pertamax Green 95 in July 2023, Indonesia's first commercially available E5 bioethanol-gasoline blend combining RON 95 gasoline with 5% bioethanol derived from sugarcane molasses sourced from local farmers in Mojokerto, East Java[4].
However, technical readiness has not translated into broad market acceptance. By the end of 2024, Pertamax Green 95 was available at approximately 160 gas stations, growing to 170 by November 2025, representing less than 2.6% of Pertamina's total retail network of roughly more than 6,000+ gas stations[5,6]. Sales data from 2023 showed only approximately 2 million liters sold in the product's first year, indicating weak consumer uptake[7]. The fuel is priced approximately 35% higher than subsidized RON 90 gasoline, which still dominates the domestic market with a share exceeding 84%[1]. This price gap, combined with entrenched consumer familiarity with conventional fuels, reflects a disconnect between policy-driven product development and actual purchasing behavior.
This pattern aligns with the broader 'attitude-behavior gap' phenomenon observed in emerging markets, where consumers express environmental concern yet continue purchasing conventional products due to price, perceived value, and brand familiarity considerations [8,9]. Meanwhile, non-Pertamina brands captured 15% of non-subsidized fuel consumption in January-July 2025, up from 11% in 2024, intensifying competitive pressure in the premium fuel segment[6]. Systemic challenges in ethanol supply chain scalability and refueling infrastructure further constrain adoption[10].
The application of the 7Ps Marketing Mix framework, Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, and Physical Evidence, offers a systematic analytical lens to diagnose these marketing gaps and design actionable strategies for Pertamax Green 95. Unlike the traditional 4Ps, the extended 7Ps model is particularly suited to service-intensive retail energy markets where customer experience and operational efficiency significantly influence purchase decisions.

1.1.2 Research Significance
This research holds both academic significance and practical application value, particularly in the context of Pertamina's role in Indonesia's energy transition.
Practical Application Value. First, the research has direct strategic implications for Pertamina as it identifies the principal obstacles in consumer willingness to adopt Pertamax Green 95, including awareness gaps, price sensitivity, performance perceptions, and brand trust challenges.
Second, using the 7Ps marketing mix framework, the research methodically examines Pertamina's current marketing execution and determines needs to enhance product communication, pricing strategy, distribution effectiveness, service quality, and promotional communication, specifically targeting the latter stages of the marketing adoption cycle where consumer conversion occurs.
Third, through STP-based market segmentation analysis, the research supports Pertamina in identifying optimal consumer segments and developing more precise positioning strategies, resulting in better product marketing resource allocation and reduced inefficient mass-market spending.
Fourth, the research enhances Pertamina's internal decision-making capability by offering a systematic, evidence-based assessment of consumer perceptions and marketing effectiveness, allowing more informed adjustments in product development, budgeting, promotional efforts, and cross-departmental collaboration.
Fifth, the research provides a replicable analytical framework combining environmental scanning, consumer insight analysis, marketing-mix evaluation, and strategy reformation, applicable to other fuel and energy companies introducing sustainable products in price-sensitive, subsidy-influenced emerging markets.
Sixth, the research supports Indonesia's energy-transition policies, particularly the national goal of decreasing carbon emissions and promoting uptake of low-emission fuels. By identifying and addressing the marketing barriers that policy mandates alone cannot overcome, the findings inform more effective public-private communication strategies and incentive design.
Theoretical Significance. First, this research contributes by applying established marketing frameworks (7Ps, STP, PESTEL, and Porter's Five Forces) to the under-researched area of green fuel marketing in emerging economies. While these frameworks have been extensively deployed in hospitality, healthcare, and financial services, their systematic application to sustainable fuel marketing in Southeast Asia remains limited[11,12].
Second, this research addresses the asymmetry between supply-side biofuel policy research and demand-side consumer marketing research. Existing Indonesian literature has predominantly focused on biodiesel mandate mechanics, foreign exchange savings, and feedstock supply chains, yet the gasoline bioethanol segment, which requires active consumer choice rather than invisible blending, has received minimal scholarly attention[2,13]. This supply-demand asymmetry, manifesting in the attitude-behavior gap where environmentally conscious consumers still purchase conventional fuels, represents the core analytical problem this thesis addresses. By examining Pertamax Green 95 as a consumer-visible product that demands awareness, willingness to pay, and distribution accessibility, this thesis fills the gap between energy policy studies and consumer marketing research.
Third, this research adds to the scant academic discussion regarding state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and green marketing. Pertamina's public ownership creates a distinctive credibility dynamic where government backing simultaneously instills trust and invites skepticism about greenwashing motives. The research examines how public ownership, regulatory constraints, and national policy alignment shape sustainability communication for a product such as Pertamax Green 95, contributing to SOE marketing theory in emerging-market contexts.
1.2 Literature Review
1.2.1 Current Research Situation in Indonesia
The academic discourse on Indonesia's biofuel program has been predominantly policy- and supply-oriented. Regression analysis of data from the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources showed that B35 biodiesel contributed 38.75% of renewable energy consumption in 2024 and generated approximately IDR 123 trillion in fossil fuel import savings[2]. Their study projected B40 could increase the renewable share to 40.7% by 2025, contingent upon stable CPO supply and US$4.1 billion in infrastructure investment. However, this research stops at the production and distribution stage, offering no insight into consumer awareness, willingness to pay, or marketing determinants of biofuel adoption.
Indonesia's biofuel policy has been examined through the circular economy lens, demonstrating that the country's biodiesel program represents one of the world's most ambitious bioenergy transition initiatives[13]. Their bibliometric analysis confirmed that global biofuel sustainability themes directly parallel Indonesia's palm oil-to-biodiesel value chain, but focused on production technology rather than demand-side consumer behavior. Analysis of B40 implementation challenges identified infrastructure readiness, feedstock supply security, and inter-ministerial coordination as critical success factors, while largely overlooking marketing and consumer adoption issues[11].
In the petroleum marketing domain, A 7Ps-based analysis of Pertamina's methanol business identified logistics gaps, limited digital platform utilization, and inconsistent service standards as key marketing challenges[14]. Comparative analysis across the United States, Germany, and Indonesia found that green marketing initiatives significantly increase sustainability awareness; however, the effect was considerably weaker in Indonesia than in developed economies[8]. This finding directly supports the present thesis's emphasis on locally adapted green marketing strategies rather than transplanted Western templates.

1.2.2 Current Research Situation Abroad
International literature provides theoretical frameworks and comparative benchmarks. Cultural values and income levels have been shown to significantly moderate consumer responsiveness to green marketing claims, with consumers in emerging markets requiring more tangible and economically rationalized messaging[9]. In biofuel consumer acceptance, Brazil's Pro-Alcool program showed that sustained government support, price competitiveness, and infrastructure availability are necessary but insufficient; consumer education and brand trust-building were equally critical[15]. In the United States, the Renewable Fuel Standard's E10 mandate achieved near-universal adoption through regulatory compulsion, while E15 adoption lagged due to vehicle compatibility concerns and retail availability constraints directly analogous to Pertamax Green 95's challenges[16]. Public acceptance of fossil fuel subsidy removal and carbon taxation in developing countries, including Indonesia, increases significantly when the use of generated revenue is communicated transparently[17].
In the 7Ps marketing mix literature, Consumers perceive green products as credible only when firms demonstrate economic responsibility alongside environmental commitment[11]. Combining green products with affordability measures and loyalty programs significantly increases adoption among middle-income households. These findings are directly applicable to Pertamax Green 95's pricing challenge, where the 35% premium over subsidized RON 90 gasoline creates the primary consumer adoption barrier identified in Section 1.1.1. Economic responsibility has been identified as a mediating factor in the relationship between green marketing initiatives and consumer purchasing behavior[18,19], this is directly relevant to Pertamax Green 95's pricing strategy in Indonesia's price-sensitive market.
Despite this growing literature, a significant research gap persists: no study has applied the 7Ps marketing mix framework to bioethanol-gasoline blends in Southeast Asian emerging markets. This thesis addresses this gap by integrating international theoretical frameworks with Indonesian market-specific primary data to design a comprehensive, 7Ps-grounded marketing strategy for Pertamax Green 95.

1.3.2 Research Methods
This study employs two complementary research methods to ensure both theoretical depth and empirical applicability: the literature research method and the questionnaire survey method.
The literature research method forms the theoretical foundation of the study. Publications on marketing strategy, sustainable and green marketing, consumer behavior, and biofuel adoption are systematically collected, evaluated, and synthesized. Sources include peer-reviewed journals such as the Journal of Marketing, Energy Policy, Journal of Cleaner Production, and Journal of Business Ethics, alongside government policy documents (ESDM) and Pertamina corporate reports. Academic databases include Web of Science, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, SAGE Journals, and CNKI. The review informs theoretical framework selection (7Ps, STP, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, SWOT) and identifies the research gap.
Questionnaire Survey Method. The questionnaire survey provides empirical data to explore consumer perceptions related to the marketing strategy of Pertamax Green 95. The research adopts a single embedded case study design, with Pertamina's Pertamax Green 95 as the unit of analysis.
A structured questionnaire is designed around the 7Ps marketing mix framework, comprising: (a) 7Ps core questions on 5-point Likert scales (1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree) across all seven dimensions; (b) satisfaction and loyalty measures (repurchase intention, recommendation willingness); (c) open-ended questions on adoption barriers and improvement suggestions; and (d) customer demographic.
Data analysis will be using descriptive statistics (frequency, percentage, mean) for demographic and 7Ps dimension profiling, importance-performance gap analysis to identify priority improvement areas, and qualitative thematic analysis for open-ended response coding. This combined approach ensures methodological consistency and practical relevance in evaluating Pertamax Green 95's marketing effectiveness.

Related Concepts and Theoretical Aspects of Marketing
2.1 Related Concepts
2.1.1 Definition of Marketing
Kotler and Keller define marketing strategy as the art and science of choosing target markets and building profitable relationships with them[20]. It encompasses selecting target market segments, establishing competitive positioning, and designing the marketing mix to serve those segments effectively. In competitive industries, marketing strategy serves as the foundation for sustainable competitive advantage by aligning organizational resources with market opportunities and customer needs[21].
The American Marketing Association (AMA) defines green marketing as the marketing of products presumed to be environmentally safe. This definition can be extended to include the holistic process of developing, promoting, and distributing products that satisfy customer needs while minimizing negative environmental impacts. In the energy sector, green marketing involves communicating ecological benefits of sustainable products to environmentally conscious consumers while addressing economic and performance concerns that influence purchase decisions in price-sensitive emerging markets[22].
2.1.2 STP Theory
STP marketing theory comprises three sequential strategic components: market segmentation, target market selection, and market positioning. Together, these components form a systematic framework for identifying, evaluating, and serving distinct consumer groups with tailored marketing approaches[20].
Market segmentation is the process of dividing a heterogeneous market into distinct subgroups of consumers with similar needs, characteristics, or behaviors. Common segmentation bases include geographic (region, climate, population density), demographic (age, income, occupation), psychographic (lifestyle, values, personality), and behavioral (purchase occasion, benefits sought, usage rate). Effective segmentation requires that segments be measurable, accessible, substantial, differentiable, and actionable.
Following segmentation, the firm evaluates each segment's attractiveness and selects one or more segments to serve. Three primary targeting strategies exist: concentrated marketing (focusing resources on a single segment), differentiated marketing (targeting multiple segments with distinct offerings), and niche marketing (specializing in a narrowly defined segment with specialized expertise). The choice depends on company resources, product homogeneity, market homogeneity, and competitor strategies.
Market positioning involves designing the company's offering and image to occupy a distinctive place in the minds of the target market. Key positioning strategies include differentiation on functional attributes (product features, performance), emotional appeals (brand personality, values), and value proposition (price-quality trade-offs). Perceptual mapping is a valuable tool for visualizing how consumers perceive competing brands related to key dimensions such as price, quality, and service.

2.1.3 7Ps Marketing Mix Theory
The 7Ps marketing-mix framework extends the traditional 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) by adding three service-oriented elements (People, Process, Physical Evidence), making it particularly applicable to service-intensive industries such as retail, hospitality, and financial services[12]. Each element represents a controllable variable that marketers can adjust to influence consumer perceptions and purchase behavior.
Product strategy encompasses all attributes that deliver value to consumers, including quality, features, design, branding, and packaging. The product can be describe as a multi-level concept comprising the core product (fundamental service), the actual product (features, quality, branding), and the augmented product (after-sales service, warranties, delivery). Effective product strategy addresses consumer needs at all three levels.
Pricing strategy involves setting price levels, discounts, allowances, and payment terms that reflect the product's value proposition while remaining competitive. Value-based pricing, which links price to perceived customer value rather than production cost, has been shown to generate superior profitability compared with cost-plus pricing approaches[23]. Pricing decisions must consider consumer price sensitivity, competitive positioning, and long-term brand equity.
Place strategy concerns distribution channel selection, coverage, logistics, and inventory management. Distribution decisions determine product accessibility and availability, directly influencing market penetration and customer convenience. The choice between direct and indirect channels, and between intensive and selective distribution, depends on product characteristics, target market behavior, and competitive dynamics.
Promotion encompasses advertising, sales promotion, public relations, and digital marketing activities that communicate the product's value proposition. Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) theory advocates for consistent messaging across all touchpoints to build brand awareness and drive purchase behavior[20].
People refer to all human actors who participate in service delivery, including front-line staff, customer service representatives, and technical support personnel. In service industries, well-trained personnel who consistently deliver high-quality service significantly influence consumer trust and repurchase intention[24,25].
Process encompasses the procedures, mechanisms, and flow of activities through which services are delivered. The process design affects both operational efficiency and customer experience, making it a critical determinant of service quality and competitive advantage.
Physical evidence comprises the tangible elements that consumers encounter during service delivery, including facility environment, signage, equipment, and employee appearance. The physical evidence serves as a credibility cue in service contexts where the core offering is intangible, helping to reduce perceived risk and build consumer confidence.

2.2 Environmental Analytical Tools
2.2.1 PESTEL Macro-Environment Analysis Model
PESTEL analysis is a macro-environmental scanning framework that examines six external dimensions shaping industry conditions: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal. It provides a structured approach for identifying opportunities and threats that influence strategic marketing decisions[26].
Political factors encompass government policies, political stability, tax policies, trade restrictions, and state intervention in markets. In regulated industries such as energy and finance, political decisions directly affect market structure, competitive dynamics, and strategic options available to firms.
Economic factors include GDP growth rates, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, and disposable income levels. These variables determine market attractiveness, consumer purchasing power, and investment feasibility for market entrants and incumbents alike.
Social factors capture demographic trends, cultural norms, lifestyle patterns, education levels, and attitudes toward sustainability. Shifting social values, particularly the growing emphasis on environmental responsibility among younger consumers, creates both opportunities and challenges for firms in consumer-facing industries[27].
Technological factors include R&D activity, automation potential, digital innovation, and technology transfer. Rapid technological change can disrupt established business models while creating new market opportunities for firms capable of adapting[11].
Environmental factors encompass climate conditions, environmental regulations, carbon emission standards, and sustainability expectations. As governments worldwide implement Net Zero commitments, environmental considerations increasingly influence consumer preferences, regulatory requirements, and corporate[1].
Legal factors include antitrust law, consumer protection regulations, employment law, health and safety standards, and industry-specific legislation. Compliance requirements affect operational costs, market entry barriers, and competitive positioning across industries.

2.2.2 Porter’s Five Forces Analysis Model
Porter's Five Forces framework analyzes industry competitive structure through five interrelated forces that determine the intensity of competition and profitability potential: threat of new entrants, bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers, threat of substitutes, and competitive rivalry among existing firms[21].
Barriers to entry determine how easily new competitors can enter an industry. High barriers include capital intensity, economies of scale, regulatory requirements, and established brand loyalty. Industries with low barriers face continuous competitive pressure from new entrants.
Supplier power increases when suppliers are concentrated, offer differentiated inputs, or control critical resources. Powerful suppliers can raise prices or reduce quality, squeezing industry profitability.
Buyer power increases when buyers are concentrated, face low switching costs, or can easily compare alternatives. In markets with high buyer power, firms must compete aggressively on price and service to retain customers.
Substitutes are alternative products or services that fulfill the same customer’s need. The threat is high when substitutes offer comparable benefits at lower prices or with superior convenience, constraining the pricing power of incumbent firms.
Rivalry intensity depends on the number of competitors, industry growth rate, product differentiation, and exit barriers. Slow-growing industries with numerous undifferentiated competitors experience the most intense rivalry.

2.2.3 SWOT Analysis Model.
SWOT analysis integrates internal organizational factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external environmental conditions (Opportunities, Threats) to generate a comprehensive strategic assessment. It synthesizes insights from macro-environmental scanning tools such as PESTEL and industry structure analysis such as Porter's Five Forces into actionable strategic directions.
Strengths and Weaknesses are internal factors over which the organization has direct control, including resources, capabilities, brand equity, operational efficiency, and human capital. Opportunities and Threats are external factors shaped by market conditions, competitive dynamics, regulatory changes, and technological shifts. The strategic value of SWOT lies in matching internal strengths to external opportunities while developing plans to overcome weaknesses and mitigate threats.
SWOT serves as a bridge between environmental analysis and strategy formulation. After PESTEL identifies macro trends and Porter's Five Forces reveals industry structure, SWOT organizes these insights relative to the firm's specific circumstances, generating four strategic postures: SO (maxi-maxi) strategies that leverage strengths to exploit opportunities, WO (mini-maxi) strategies that overcome weaknesses to capture opportunities, ST (maxi-mini) strategies that use strengths to avoid threats, and WT (mini-mini) strategies that minimize weaknesses and avoid threats.

3.1 Overview of PT Pertamina (Persero)
3.1.1 Corporate Background and Energy Transition Mission
PT Pertamina (Persero) is Indonesia's state-owned integrated energy conglomerate, established in 1968 through the merger of Pertamin and Permina. As the national energy company, Pertamina operates across the full hydrocarbon value chain, employing approximately 35,000 people directly and supporting millions of indirect livelihoods across the national economy. PT Pertamina Patra Niaga, the Subholding Commercial & Trading (SH C&T), serves as the downstream commercial and retail arm responsible for fuel distribution, trading, and retail operations through Indonesia's largest gas station network[28].
In 2024, Pertamina achieved fuel sales of 105,052,452 kl, representing 5.6% year-on-year growth from 99,464,656 KL in 2023. Financial performance exceeded targets, with profit reaching USD 1.1 billion (103.2% of target) and EBITDA at USD 2.0 billion (121.1% of target). The company's health rating stands at AAA (idn) (Stable), with ESG rating of BBB and Good Corporate Governance (GCG) score of 83.13. Operational safety metrics include a Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR) of 0.05 in 2024, well below the tolerance threshold of 0.11. The company has maintained consistent environmental compliance, recognized through multiple PROPER awards from the Ministry of Environment and Forestry[28].
Pertamina's energy transition mission is anchored in the Net Zero Emission 2060 target, operationalized through a dual-pillar strategy: (1) green business development encompassing biofuels, renewable energy, and new energy sources; and (2) decarbonization of existing operations. Pertamax Green 95 falls within the first pillar, representing the company's first commercial-scale bioethanol-gasoline blend for the retail automotive market. The product supports multiple government mandates including Peraturan Menteri ESDM No. 12 Tahun 2015 on biofuel utilization and Indonesia's Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) under the Paris Agreement.
3.1.2 Business Structure and Strategic Objectives
Pertamina 's organizational structure operates through three primary business segments: (1) Fuel Retail, managing approximately more than 6,000+ gas station nationwide; (2) Fuel Trading, serving industrial, commercial, and marine customers; and (3) Logistics & Infrastructure, encompassing fuel storage depots, transportation fleets, and supply chain management. The Fuel Retail division is organized by regional sales territories including SA Jabodetabek, SA Banten, SA Karawang, SA Cirebon, SA Bandung, SA Semarang, SA Tegal, SA Yogyakarta, SA Surabaya, SA Kediri, and SA Malang, enabling localized distribution planning and market responsiveness.
The company's strategic objectives for 2024-2028 center on three pillars: operational excellence (maintaining fuel supply security across 17,000+ islands), commercial growth (defending market share against escalating non-Pertamina competition, which reached 15% of non-subsidized fuel consumption in 2025), and energy transition acceleration (scaling Pertamax Green 95 and future biofuel products).

3.2 Current Marketing Strategy Analysis
This section analyzes the current marketing strategy for Pertamax Green 95 using 7Ps framework[29]. The analysis documents the implementation status, operational scope, and preliminary performance indicators for each marketing mix. 3.2.1 Product Strategy
Pertamax Green 95 is Indonesia's first commercial-scale bioethanol-gasoline blend for the retail market, launched on June 20, 2023. The product combines RON 95 gasoline with 5% bioethanol (E5) derived from sugarcane molasses sourced from East Java. The bioethanol is produced by PT Enero with a supply capacity of approximately 30,000 kiloliters per year, blended at Pertamina's fuel terminals before distribution to retail gas station. The product holds SK Dirjen Migas ESDM No. 252.k/HK.02/DJM/2023 as its official specification certificate and has been tested and validated by LEMIGAS (Lembaga Minyak dan Gas Bumi).
The product is positioned as a hybrid performance-eco fuel, targeting environmentally conscious private vehicle owners who prioritize engine compatibility alongside reduced carbon emissions. This positioning bridges the conventional performance fuel segment (Pertamax RON 92) and the premium performance segment (Pertamax Turbo RON 98), differentiating through its E5 bioethanol content and ESG alignment. The product's three-layer value architecture consists of: (1) core product, engine fuel delivering RON 95 combustion performance; (2) actual product, bioethanol-blended gasoline with certified quality and regulatory compliance; and (3) augmented product, environmental sustainability attributes, agricultural community support, and national energy transition contribution.

3.2.2 Price Strategy
Pertamax Green 95's pricing reflects a market-building approach rather than profit maximization. The product is positioned at a price point at or below Pertamax Turbo and, at select gas stations, priced competitively with conventional Pertamax. This pricing architecture reflects strategic margin absorption by Pertamina, accepting lower per-unit profitability to accelerate consumer trial and market share acquisition.
The pricing flexibility is enabled by Peraturan Menteri ESDM No. 62 Tahun 2020 on Biofuel Retail Pricing, which permits Pertamina to set non-subsidized biofuel prices within a specified band relative to the HIP (Harga Indeks Pasar) gasoline reference price, provided the retail price does not exceed the ceiling price for equivalent-octane fossil fuel. This regulatory framework allows Pertamina to absorb the bioethanol cost premium without violating price regulations.

3.2.4 Promotion Strategy
Promotional activities for Pertamax Green 95 currently rely primarily on organic social media posts on Pertamina's official channels and corporate communications materials distributed across sales areas. Digital media content highlighting environmental benefits is produced on a limited basis, and event-based product showcases occur at select corporate functions and sustainability forums. No dedicated advertising campaign or consumer education program exists for the product.
The current promotional approach creates a notable imbalance: distribution has expanded to 177 gas station without proportional investment in demand generation. As noted in internal strategic reviews, outlet growth is not proportional to sales growth. The addition of 70 new gas station between January and November 2025 did not generate a corresponding sales increase, suggesting that distribution expansion without accompanying promotional support may not effectively drive adoption.
Three promotional areas are currently underdeveloped: (1) consumer education, no systematic campaign explains bioethanol content, E5 blending benefits, or environmental impact; (2) competitive differentiation, no advertising positions Pertamax Green 95 against Non-Pertamina brands (Shell V-Power) or conventional Pertamax on performance or sustainability dimensions; and (3) trial inducement, no promotional pricing, sampling programs, or loyalty incentives specifically target first-time users. The product relies on organic brand equity from the Pertamax parent brand and incidental exposure through corporate sustainability communications.
3.2.5 People Strategy
The People dimension encompasses two stakeholder groups: frontline gas station personnel (pump attendants, cashiers, supervisors) and customer service representatives (call centers, social media managers, MyPertamina support).
Frontline staff training programs currently focus on standard refueling procedures and digital payment operations, with product-specific modules for Pertamax Green 95 integrated on an ad-hoc basis. When consumers ask questions about bioethanol content, engine compatibility, or product differences, frontline staff may not consistently provide detailed answers. Research on MyPertamina implementation found that Pertamina officers do not fully understand the digital applications they support, a finding that may also apply to frontline knowledge of biofuel products[31].
Gas station staff performance metrics focus on transaction volume, fuel sales revenue, and customer service speed. Performance-based incentives for green fuel sales are not currently part of the compensation structure. Customer service channels (MyPertamina app, call center 135, social media) similarly lack dedicated training modules on Pertamax Green 95. The MyPertamina app does not feature a dedicated product information section, green fuel locator function, or loyalty program differentiation for green fuel purchases[31].
3.2.6 Process Strategy
The current purchase process follows a standardized three-step sequence: (1) pump selection via nozzle labeling, (2) payment through cash, QRIS, or MyPertamina wallet, and (3) receipt generation. No dedicated green-fuel verification or post-transaction environmental impact notification is currently integrated into digital or physical workflow.
For pre-purchase information search, consumers seeking Pertamax Green 95 availability face a fragmented discovery experience. Pertamina website provides basic product information (specifications, FAQ, environmental claims), but no interactive gas station locator filters for Green 95 availability. The MyPertamina app enables general gas station location search but does not identify which gas stations stock Pertamax Green 95. This information gap requires consumers to call individual gas stations or physically visit stations to check availability.
Post-purchase engagement is not systematically structured for Pertamax Green 95 consumers. There is no feedback mechanism, follow-up communication about environmental impact, or loyalty program tier differentiation for green fuel customers. This represents a gap in developing emotional attachment and repeat-purchase behavior through transparent communication of environmental outcomes[17].
3.2.7 Physical Evidence Strategy
Physical evidence encompasses all tangible cues consumers encounter at gas station. For Pertamax Green 95, current physical evidence consists primarily of basic product labeling on dispenser nozzles, with limited green-specific branding at the station level. The overall gas station environment provides minimal green credibility cues beyond standard pump labeling.
Internal assessments and the Customer Satisfaction and Loyalty Survey 2024 indicate that while standard gas station facilities meet baseline expectations, green-specific physical evidence is largely absent[32]. There are no prominent environmental certification displays, visual communication of bioethanol content and benefits, dedicated green canopy or station-zone branding, or interactive displays explaining environmental impact. A survey involving 469 Pertamax consumers at gas station locations in Jakarta confirmed that environmental attitudes mediate the relationship between consumer satisfaction and repeat-purchase behavior[33].

3.3 Consumer Research Analysis
3.3.1 Survey Methods
The consumer research component employs a qualitative survey methodology designed to capture the attitudes, perceptions, and behavioral intentions of private vehicle owners in major Indonesian cities regarding Pertamax Green 95. The survey instrument is administered through an online questionnaire distributed via Google Forms, selected for its accessibility, cost-effectiveness, and capacity to reach geographically dispersed respondents across multiple urban centers. The questionnaire is designed for completion within 10 to 15 minutes.
The sampling frame comprises private car owners residing in major Indonesian cities including Jabodetabek (Jakarta, Bogor, Depok, Tangerang, Bekasi), Surabaya, Bandung, and Medan, selected for their representativeness of urban fuel consumption patterns, vehicle density, and variation in environmental awareness levels. Jakarta and Surabaya were prioritized because they represent Pertamina’s two primary distribution territories (Integrated Terminal Jakarta and Integrated Terminal Surabaya) and collectively account for most Pertamax Green 95 sales. Bandung and Medan were included to capture consumer perspectives from secondary growth cities with distinct demographic and psychographic profiles.
Purposive sampling was employed, targeting respondents who met the following criteria: (1) age 18–65 years; (2) own or regularly operate a gasoline-powered vehicle (car or motorcycle); (3) purchased fuel at a Pertamina’s Gas Station within the past 3–6 months; (4) aware of Pertamax Green 95 (either as current users or as non-users with product awareness); and (5) residing in one of the four target cities. This purposive approach ensures that respondents possess sufficient product exposure to provide informed assessments across all 7Ps dimensions, while the inclusion of both users and aware non-users captures the perspectives of consumers at different stages of the adoption funnel.
The questionnaire is structured around the 7Ps Marketing Mix framework as the primary analytical architecture, with four distinct sections comprising 35 closed-ended questions and 3 open-ended questions (total 38 items). Section A contains the core 7Ps questions measured on 5-point Likert scales (1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly Agree). Overall Satisfaction and Loyalty (Section B).
Beyond the 7Ps diagnostic, three outcome measures were assessed to evaluate the overall health of the consumer-brand relationship: overall satisfaction (B1), recommendation willingness (B2), and repurchase intention (B3). Based on the behavioral intentions framework and the American Customer Satisfaction Index approach, these measures were analyzed both individually and as a composite loyalty construct[40,43].
Customer Satisfaction Analysis. The overall satisfaction score of 3.677 on a 5-point scale (73.5% of maximum) positions Pertamax Green 95 in the "moderately satisfied" zone. This is consistent with the 7Ps diagnostic: consumers appreciate operational delivery (P6 Process = 3.869, P7 Physical Evidence = 3.851) but are dissatisfied with promotional communication (P4 Promotion = 2.848) and price-value perception (P2 Price = 3.079). The satisfaction score does not reach the 4.0 "enthusiastic endorsement" benchmark, indicating that positive perceptions have not yet been crystallized into emotional brand attachment.
Recommendation Willingness Distribution. The recommendation willingness scores (B2, 1-10 scale) yield a mean of 6.927 with a left-skewed distribution: 32.3% are detractors (scores 1-6), 47.6% are passives (scores 7-8), and 20.2% are promoters (scores 9-10). The result corresponds to a Net Promoter Score (NPS) of -12.1%[44]. Although NPS has recognized limitations in predicting future behavior within low-penetration markets, the negative score indicates that the brand currently has more vocal critics than advocates[45]. For a product with 2.6% retail penetration, this is expected most detractors have not purchased the product, making their recommendation score hypothetical rather than experiential. Nonetheless, the skew warrants attention: the priority is converting passives (B2 passive = 47.6%) to promoters through availability expansion and evidence-based green communication.
Repurchase Intention Analysis. The repurchase intention score of 3.427 reveals a loyalty gap: satisfaction (B1 mean = 3.677) exceeds repurchase intent (B3 mean = 3.427) by 0.250 points. This gap indicates that satisfied consumers face friction, likely availability constraints and price sensitivity that prevents satisfaction from translating into behavioral commitment. Among current users (n=67), repurchase intention rises confirming that the loyalty gap is concentrated among non-users.
Composite Loyalty Index (CLI). To integrate satisfaction and behavioral intention into a single metric, the CLI is calculated as the meaning of B1 and B3: CLI = (3.677 + 3.427) / 2 = 3.552. This value classifies the consumer relationship as "Moderate Loyalty" with a 0.448-point gap to the "Strong Loyalty" threshold (4.0). The CLI provides a tracking metric for strategy implementation.
Strategic Implications. Three loyalty diagnostics emerge: (1) the satisfaction level is adequate but not enthusiastic; (2) the loyalty gap (satisfaction > repurchase intent) identifies availability and price as conversion barriers; (3) the large passive segment (47.6%) represents the highest-ROI conversion target. These findings converge with the 7Ps diagnostic: closing the Promotion and Place gaps will have the largest impact on loyalty improvement.
Open-Ended Responses (Section C).
Three open-ended questions (C1: barriers, C2: convincing factors, C3: strategic suggestions) generated 372 qualitative responses from 124 respondents. Data were analyzed using inductive thematic coding based on a six-phase thematic analysis framework[46].
C1: Barriers to Regular Use: Responses to "What is your main barrier to using Pertamax Green 95 regularly?" (n=124) were coded into four themes:
Theme 1: Availability and Physical Access (60.5%, n=75). The dominant barrier is physical unavailability. Representative responses include: "Nearly unavailable at nearby gas stations. That's my main barrier" (R69); "None sold near home. Hard to buy regularly" (R11, R30, R40 -- 11 total mentions of this exact formulation). This theme converges with the quantitative finding that P3.1 (availability at gas stations visited, mean = 2.210) is the lowest-scoring item in the entire survey. The barrier is geographic rather than economic: consumers who cannot find Pertamax Green 95 at their regular gas station have no practical choice but to use alternative fuels.
Theme 2: Price and Cost Concerns (21.0%, n=26). The second most frequent barrier is centered on price premium relative to lower-octane alternatives. Representative responses: "Regular use would be tough. Much pricier than what I currently use" (R25); "Price gap with Pertalite is too big for daily motorcycle users" (R3, R52, R84 -- 5 total mentions). This theme is income-segmented: all 26 price-related responses came from respondents earning below 20 million IDR/month. No respondent above 50 million IDR mentioned price as a barrier.
Theme 3: Awareness and Information Deficit (11.3%, n=14). A smaller but significant group reports insufficient exposure to Pertamax Green 95 marketing. Representative: "Rarely see Pertamax Green 95 ads or promos. Not enough exposure" (R47, R60, R81 -- 4 mentions). This theme validates the quantitative finding that P4.1 (advertisement visibility, mean = 2.113) is the weakest item across all 32 survey items.
Theme 4: Brand Inertia and Loyalty (7.3%, n=9). A residual theme captures habitual resistance to switching. Representative: "Just inertia. Comfortable with my old choice despite knowing Pertamax Green 95 exists" (R85); "Long loyal to another brand. Need stronger reason to switch" (R23, R28). These responses suggest that without a compelling differentiation signal, consumers default to established fuel choices.
C2: Factors That Would Drive Adoption: Responses to "What would convince you to choose Pertamax Green 95?" (n=124) reveal four convincing factors:
Theme 1: Environmental Evidence and Transparency (39.5%, n=49). The single largest convincing factor is scientific proof of environmental benefits. Representative: "Show real data on better fuel efficiency or lower emissions. Then I'll believe" (R3); "Simple, easy-to-understand scientific data on emission savings would convince me" (R59, R75); "Give a free trial with emission comparison data I can see myself" (R22, R47). This finding directly contradicts the initial quantitative impression that green positioning is weak. Qualitative analysis reveals that consumers are not rejecting green claims -- they are demanding evidence to support them. The 49 respondents (39.5%) who mention environmental evidence represent a larger group than those citing price incentives (37.9%) or availability (35.5%).
Theme 2: Price Incentives and Discounts (37.9%, n=47). The second-largest convincing factor group requests financial incentives for trial. Representative: "Just give me a first-time free promo or 10% discount. I'll try and maybe continue" (R54); "A reward points program redeemable for free fuel would make me buy more often" (R5, R21, R39 -- 3 mentions). Notably, availability is cited as a convincing factor (35.5%) less frequently than as a barrier (60.5%), suggesting that consumers distinguish between "hard barriers" (can't find it) and "soft enablers" (would be used if nearby).

A residual group requests educational outreach: "An educational campaign explaining benefits in simple language would help" (R68); "Testimonials from mechanics or official workshops on engine benefits would convince me" (R13, R21). These refinements inform the strategy recommendations in Chapter 5.

This chapter examines the external and internal environments that shape Pertamax Green 95’s strategic position in Indonesia. The analysis draws exclusively on secondary data sources, including government policy documents, industry reports, financial disclosures, and publicly available corporate information
4.1 Macro-Environment Analysis (PESTEL)
The PESTEL framework systematically evaluates six dimensions of the macro-environment that influence the strategic context of Pertamax Green 95 in Indonesia[26].
4.1.1 Political Environment
The Indonesian government’s commitment to energy transition and biofuel development provides a favorable political foundation for Pertamax Green 95. Under Presidential Decree 15/2024, the government targets 1.2 billion liters of sugarcane ethanol production by 2030 and has established a dedicated bioethanol acceleration task force to expand feedstock cultivation[49]. Energy Minister Bahlil Lahadalia has announced plans for an E10 bioethanol mandate by 2027, which would elevate bioethanol-blended fuel from a niche market segment to a regulatory compliance requirement. Pertamina’s status as a state-owned enterprise ensures institutional alignment with national energy policy, providing preferential access to regulatory programs and implicit political backing for biofuel initiatives.
However, political risk remains. The E10 implementing regulation had not been issued as of late 2024, and historical precedent, the 2010 bioethanol mandate was discontinued due to inadequate feedstock supply demonstrates that policy reversal is possible. Additionally, as SOE, Pertamina faces implicit political pressure to maintain fuel affordability, which constrains pricing flexibility for premium products such as Pertamax Green 95.

4.1.2 Economic Environment
Indonesia’s macroeconomic fundamentals provide a broadly supportive context for the premium fuel segment. 4.2.1 Competitors
Rivalry in Indonesia’s premium gasoline segment is intense. Non-Pertamina brands (i.e Shell) is Pertamax Green 95’s most direct competitor, with approximately 200 retail gas stations strategically located in high-income urban areas (Jakarta, Surabaya, Bandung). Non-Pertamina brands (i.e. Shell V-Power) leverages decades of Formula 1 motorsport marketing and consistent premium positioning to target affluent consumers. Pertamax Green 95 also faces internal cannibalization from Pertamina’s own lower-tier fuels. Pertalite (RON 90) and standard Pertamax (RON 92) satisfy basic transportation needs at lower prices and are available at the same gas station network, meaning consumers have no location-based incentive to trade up. The fuel retail market exhibits the structural characteristics that intensify rivalry: limited product differentiation, low switching costs, and price-sensitive consumers. Without clear value differentiation communicated through effective marketing, consumers default to the lowest-price acceptable alternative.
4.2.2 New Entrants
The threat of standalone new fuel retailers is low due to three barriers. First, capital requirements are prohibitive: establishing a nationwide fuel retail network requires investment in storage terminals, distribution logistics, and retail real estate that runs into billions of dollars. Second, regulatory barriers are significant: the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) controls fuel import quotas, blending specifications, and retail licensing, favoring incumbents with established regulatory relationships. Third, Pertamina’s 6,000+ gas station network creates a distribution moat that new entrants cannot realistically replicate.
However, indirect entry from adjacent sectors poses a more significant threat. E-commerce platforms are entering via app-based fuel delivery services, while EV charging infrastructure operators (PLN, private networks) are capturing mobility energy expenditure. With Indonesia targeting 2 million EV two-wheelers and 600,000 EV cars by 2030, supported by tax incentives and charging infrastructure investment, the effective competitive landscape is expanding beyond traditional fuel retailers.

Buyer power in Indonesia’s fuel retail market is high. The seven strategy components are designed as an integrated system rather than independent interventions, with each element justified by both Chapter 3 consumer evidence and Chapter 4 environmental analysis. People training and Process enhancement can run concurrently from months 2-6, operating at the service encounter level and requiring distribution infrastructure to be in place first. Research Conclusions and Future Prospects
7.1 Main Research Conclusions
This thesis examined the marketing strategy of Pertamax Green 95 in the Indonesian market through environmental analysis (PESTEL, Porter’s Five Forces, SWOT), consumer survey research (n = 124), and strategy design (STP and 7Ps). The objective of the research was to diagnose the marketing failures underlying the product’s weak market acceptance after 28 months of availability, and to design an evidence-based marketing strategy.
The macro-environmental analysis confirmed favorable structural conditions for green fuel marketing. Presidential Regulation No. 66/2018 mandates bioethanol blending as part of the national energy mix, the Net Zero Emission 2060 target creates long-term policy support, and biofuel programs generated US$3.68 billion in foreign exchange savings in the first half of 2025 alone. However, the micro-environment revealed intensifying competitive pressure: non-Pertamina brands increased their share of non-subsidized fuel from 11% in 2024 to 15% in 2025. The SWOT analysis confirmed that Pertamina’s 6,000+-gas station network and brand recognition are core strengths, but promotional underinvestment and distribution gaps are critical weaknesses that competitors are exploiting. The favorable macro-environment creates a strategic window, but without corrective marketing action, Pertamina risks ceding the green premium segment to non-Pertamina brands with stronger consumer engagement.
The survey analysis revealed that Pertamax Green 95’s core problem is marketing activation failure, not product failure. The overall 7Ps mean score of 3.449 (Moderate) conceals a profound asymmetry: Process (P6 = 3.869) and Physical Evidence (P7 = 3.851) rank as Good, while Promotion (P4 = 2.848) is the sole dimension classified as Needs Improvement. Five interrelated problems were identified: promotional failure and brand invisibility (P4.1 = 2.113, the lowest of 32 items, with 44.4% of respondents unaware or untried); price-value perception asymmetry (P2.3 = 2.637); distribution coverage deficiency (P3.1 = 2.210, with 60.5% citing availability as the primary barrier); digital marketing underutilization; and green value proposition communication failure (39.5% of respondents explicitly requesting environmental evidence). Promotional deficiency operates as the foundational bottleneck: without awareness, distribution expansion cannot generate trial, and price incentives cannot stimulate demand.
The STP analysis identified four integrated segments. The Green-Affluent Urban Professionals segment (age 25–44, income 10–50 million IDR, high environmental concern, 16.1% share) was selected as the primary target due to its intersection of purchasing power and green predisposition. The targeting strategy prioritizes retention of current users (54.0%) over acquisition of unaware non-users (21.8%), with aware non-triers (22.6%) representing the highest-ROI conversion opportunity. Market positioning centers on the uncontested “green premium” space: certified RON 95 performance with verified environmental benefits at a mid-market price, differentiated by the Indonesian bioethanol content and Mojokerto farmer community narrative that no competitor can .
The integrated 7P strategy was designed as a coordinated execution system. Promotion strategy, the highest-priority intervention, rebuilds around digital content leadership (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok), an influencer Ambassador Program (20–30 creators), and point-of-purchase awareness panels at 500 non-stocking gas station. Place strategy targets 30% network penetration (approximately 1,950 gas station) within 24 months with phased expansion beyond Java. Price strategy employs a Green Value Guarantee via MyPertamina, first-fill trial incentives (10–15% voucher), and bundled pricing for price-sensitive segments. Product strategy commissions a LEMIGAS-validated performance study to close the engine perception gap (P1.2 = 3.177). People strategy deploys the Pertamax Green 95 Ambassador Certification program for frontline staff. Process strategy enhances MyPertamina with a Green Impact Receipt and gamification. Physical Evidence strategy establishes the Green Zone concept at all Pertamax Green 95 gas station.

The strategy is projected to improve the overall 7Ps mean score from 3.449 toward 3.65–3.70 over 12 months, raise the Composite Loyalty Index from 3.552 toward 3.80, and move NPS from −12.1% toward positive territory. The strategy contributes to customer acquisition by resolving awareness and availability barriers blocking 44.4% of consumers; to customer satisfaction by closing the 0.250-point satisfaction-repurchase gap; to brand positioning by establishing Pertamax Green 95’s unique green premium identity; to market competitiveness by matching non-Pertamina brands’ digital presence; and to long-term sustainable development by building the awareness, distribution, and brand equity required for Pertamina to lead Indonesia’s E5-to-E10 bioethanol transition.
7.2 Future Prospects
With the government’s E10 bioethanol mandate targeted for 2027 and bioethanol consumption projected to grow toward 52.3 billion liters by 2050, Pertamina has a time-sensitive opportunity to transition Pertamax Green 95 from a niche offering into the leading brand in the non-subsidized retail fuel market. Realizing this transition requires addressing structural constraints in parallel with marketing execution: accelerating bioethanol supply chain development with bioethanol supplier and government agricultural agencies, expanding distribution infrastructure to outer-island markets, and building dedicated digital marketing capability within the commercial organization.
Driven by Indonesia’s energy transition mission, the progressive E10 mandate trajectory, and the growing environmental consciousness of the urban middle-class consumer segment, Pertamax Green 95 is well-positioned to become a foundational case study in green fuel adoption across Southeast Asia. Its unique combination of domestic bioethanol origin, Mojokerto farmer community narrative, government policy alignment, and LEMIGAS-certified environmental credentials provides competitive differentiation that foreign-brand competitors cannot . Future research should expand geographic and sample coverage to outer-island markets, adopt longitudinal designs to track adoption behavior as the Chapter 5 strategies are implemented, and apply structural equation modeling to validate the causal relationships between 7P dimensions and customer loyalty identified in this study. Comparative research across the Philippines, Vietnam, and Thailand would further assess whether the marketing activation failure pattern identified here generalizes to biofuel products in other price-sensitive emerging markets, contributing to the cross-national green marketing theory.
